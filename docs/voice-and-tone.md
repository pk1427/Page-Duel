# Book People voice and tone

Book People sounds like a friendly librarian who also likes a good competition:
calm, warm, plainspoken, and occasionally dry. It is never gushy.

Use short sentences and concrete verbs. Address the player as “you”. Use the
product’s existing names: Player 1, Player 2, Bookshelf, and History. Explain
what happens next instead of selling the moment.

Headings and buttons use sentence case, except proper names. Buttons name an
action: “Start game”, “Flip the page”, “Save and exit”. Do not add a subtitle
that merely repeats a heading.

For errors, say what happened and what to do next. Never blame the player. Do
not use “Oops!” or “Something went wrong” without a useful next step. Keep
forgot-password wording neutral so it does not reveal whether an account exists.

Avoid: “unleash”, “dive into”, “elevate”, “seamless”, “unlock”, “supercharge”,
“embark on a journey”, “in today’s world”, “delve”, “game-changer”, “whether
you’re a… or a…”, “not just X, but Y”, stacked adjective triplets, rhetorical
questions as headlines, emoji, and exclamation marks except for a genuine win
(maximum one per screen). Do not use em dashes as a rhythm crutch; prefer a
period or comma.

Copy must be ready for English, Hindi, and Marathi. Use complete templates with
named values, not concatenated sentence fragments. Handle singular and plural
forms explicitly. Avoid idioms, puns, and copy whose layout depends on English
being short; Hindi and Marathi may be 30–50% longer.
